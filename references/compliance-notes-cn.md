# 中国内地 / 北京临时团队合规提示

This reference is a practical checklist, not legal, tax, HR, or accounting advice. Use it to flag issues and recommend professional confirmation when stakes are high. When the user needs a current legal, tax, HR, platform, or Beijing policy conclusion, verify current official sources before making a specific claim.

## 1. 成果交付 vs 变相用工

Prefer outcome-based service when the user wants a temporary collaborator:

- Define deliverables, acceptance criteria, deadline, fee, change rules, confidentiality, and copyright/use rights.
- Avoid controlling how, when, and where the collaborator works unless a specific safety or handoff requirement exists.
- Avoid long-term fixed attendance, exclusive management, daily command, salary-like payment, disciplinary control, and indefinite scope.

Risk signals that may need HR/legal review:

- The person works continuously for the user like a staff member.
- The user sets daily working hours, attendance, leave rules, and direct task discipline.
- The person is economically and operationally dependent on the user.
- Work has no clear deliverable, only "be available and do whatever comes up."

Safer wording:

```text
本合作以成果交付为目标，验收依据为双方确认的交付物和验收标准。承接方自主安排具体工作方式、工具和时间；委托方不设置考勤、请假、纪律处分或持续坐班要求。
```

## 2. 非全日制、兼职、劳务派遣

If the user is actually arranging part-time/offline work, distinguish it from outcome-based service.

- Under the PRC Labor Contract Law, non-full-time employment is generally paid by the hour, with average daily working time for the same employer not exceeding 4 hours and cumulative weekly working time not exceeding 24 hours.
- Labor dispatch is a regulated model and should be handled through qualified dispatch entities. The Labor Contract Law frames dispatch as a supplemental form, generally for temporary, auxiliary, or substitute positions.
- Do not design "temporary team" language to evade employment obligations.

## 3. Human Resources Service Boundary

If the workflow starts matching candidates, collecting resumes, charging placement fees, or operating like a recruitment agency, it may touch regulated human resources service activity.

For this skill, stay on the safer side:

- Help the founder write task briefs, selection criteria, trial tasks, and acceptance checklists.
- Recommend channel categories and due-diligence questions.
- Do not claim to be a licensed recruiter, dispatch provider, or employment intermediary.

## 4. New Employment Forms And Platform Work

For couriers, repair workers, ride-hailing drivers, delivery riders, and similar platform workers, avoid treating them as invisible labor. The State Council / ministries have policy direction around protecting new-employment-form workers' labor rights.

When a temporary team uses platform workers:

- Respect platform and worker safety rules.
- Do not ask for unsafe speed, illegal parking, dangerous delivery, or unpaid waiting.
- Pay for extra waiting, long distance, materials, or changed scope when appropriate.
- Communicate clearly and politely; do not push algorithmic pressure further down to the person doing the work.
- If the task is handled through a platform order, respect the platform's payment, safety, complaint, and after-sales process instead of pushing the worker to bypass the recorded process.

## 5. Personal Information And Data

Use data minimization:

- Share only the minimum data needed for the task.
- De-identify customer, student, patient, phone, ID, address, payment, and contract information whenever possible.
- Clarify purpose, scope, access, retention, and deletion.
- Avoid uploading sensitive or personal data into AI tools without a lawful basis, necessity, and user approval.

For AI workers:

- Prefer synthetic, masked, or summarized data.
- Ask the AI to work on structure, templates, checks, or dummy examples before using real data.
- Have a human review AI output before external delivery.
- Keep a record of what data was shared, with whom, for what purpose, and when it should be deleted or returned.

## 6. Copyright, Use Rights, And Source Materials

For design, writing, code, video, PPT, brand, photography, and AI-assisted output:

- State whether the founder receives copyright assignment or only a usage license.
- State where the work may be used: internal, commercial, public marketing, ads, resale, client delivery, education, etc.
- State whether the collaborator can show the work as a case.
- Require disclosure or permission for third-party materials, fonts, photos, templates, plugins, code libraries, and AI-generated assets.
- Do not assume "paid means all rights are transferred." Put the intended rights in the task brief or agreement.
- For AI-generated or AI-assisted materials, keep source prompts, source assets, human edits, and third-party material notes when the output will be used commercially or delivered to a client.

## 7. Payment, Invoice, And Tax

Use staged payment when risk is high:

- Small deposit or kickoff payment.
- Milestone payment after accepted draft.
- Final payment after complete handoff.

For professional providers, ask about invoice ability and contract subject. For individuals, avoid cash-like ambiguity when a record is needed. For larger or recurring work, suggest accounting/tax confirmation.

## 8. Beijing Practical Notes

For Beijing local work:

- Confirm exact address, building access, equipment, start/end time, meal/transport arrangement, safety constraints, emergency contact, and backup person.
- For events, check venue rules, insurance/safety needs, on-site leader, and whether temporary staff can enter restricted areas.
- For computer repair, installation, field collection, home visit, or school-related work, protect privacy and personal safety. Do not send unverified strangers into sensitive spaces without identity, scope, and permission.
- For school, child-related, medical, elder-care, community, home-visit, or customer-device work, raise the verification standard: identity, authorization, access boundary, data boundary, emergency contact, and written confirmation should be clearer than ordinary office tasks.
- Beijing traffic, distance, venue entry, security checks, and weather can materially affect offline delivery. Build waiting, transit, and backup time into the work order instead of treating delays as the worker's personal fault.

## 9. Human-Centered Collaboration Floor

Use this floor even when the user is under budget pressure:

- No hidden production disguised as "trial."
- No unlimited revisions.
- No 24-hour availability unless paid and explicitly agreed.
- No task expansion through emotional pressure.
- No unsafe speed, illegal shortcuts, unpaid waiting, or unrecorded platform bypass.
- No unnecessary exposure of customer, student, patient, identity, address, payment, contract, or private-device data.
- No "AI can do it cheaper, so humans should accept unreasonable prices" framing.
